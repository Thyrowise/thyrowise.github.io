How long to wait after levothyroxine before coffee?

Take it on an empty stomach and wait 30 to 60 minutes before breakfast or coffee. Keep calcium, iron, magnesium and antacids at least four hours away.

The short answer

Food: 30 to 60 minutes. Coffee: an hour. Levothyroxine is absorbed best on an empty stomach, so it is taken first thing and breakfast is held back. Coffee is given its own, longer interval because it interferes with absorption directly rather than merely breaking the fast.

Calcium and iron supplements: 2 to 4 hours, and they are the ones people most often get wrong.

Sources: Levothyroxine — MedlinePlus Drug Information, U.S. National Library of Medicine; Levothyroxine Interactions with Food and Dietary Supplements — A Systematic Review.

Why coffee gets its own rule

Coffee is not just the first thing that breaks the fast. It interferes with levothyroxine specifically, and the effect has been measured.

In the study that established this, espresso swallowed together with the tablet lowered the rise in serum T4 by 36% on average in thyroid patients and 29% in healthy volunteers, with peak levels down 30% and 19%. The proposed mechanism is that coffee sequesters the hormone in the gut, leaving less of it available to be taken up.

Sources: Benvenga S et al., Altered intestinal absorption of L-thyroxine caused by coffee, Thyroid 2008;18(3):293-301; figures as reported in Levothyroxine Interactions with Food and Dietary Supplements — A Systematic Review.

That is why the guidance for coffee is an hour, not the 30 minutes that is often enough for food. The same review notes that liquid levothyroxine formulations did not show a significant interaction with coffee — if the hour is genuinely impossible for you, that is a question worth putting to your doctor rather than something to solve on your own.

The four-hour rule is a different rule

Some things need much more separation than breakfast does. Keep calcium, iron, magnesium and antacids at least 4 hours away from a levothyroxine dose — they block absorption rather than merely slowing it.

Source: Levothyroxine — MedlinePlus Drug Information, U.S. National Library of Medicine.

This is the one people get wrong most often, because a calcium or iron supplement feels like part of the same morning routine as the thyroid tablet. If both are daily, the usual fix is to move the supplement to the evening.

Consistency beats precision

The dose you are on was arrived at by measuring your TSH some weeks after a change — usually 6 to 12 weeks after — while you were taking the tablet in whatever way you take it.

Source: Hypothyroidism, American Thyroid Association.

That means a routine you can actually keep is worth more than an ideal one you follow twice a week. If 60 minutes without coffee is not going to survive a Tuesday, a reliable 30 is the better answer — and if you take it at night instead, well after your last meal, that is a routine too. The failure mode is not "wrong window", it is a different window every day, because then a TSH result has nothing stable to be read against.

The trap nobody warns you about: the morning drink

The calcium tablet is easy to spot. The thing that actually catches people is breakfast in a carton — a meal-replacement, protein or fibre drink taken on the way out the door, at exactly the wrong moment.

The reason is simpler than a special interaction: it is food, and it is arriving at the exact moment the tablet needs an empty stomach. On top of that, MedlinePlus names soybeans, walnuts and dietary fibre as things that can affect how levothyroxine works for you — so a carton with added fibre or soy protein is doing more than just breaking the fast.

Milk as food is a smaller problem than a calcium tablet

These are two different sizes of the same effect, and it is worth keeping them apart.

Calcium and iron supplements need 2 to 4 hours of separation; MedlinePlus gives at least 4 for calcium carbonate and ferrous sulfate. Milk as food is measurable but much smaller: in the one study of it, taking levothyroxine with 355 mL of 2% cow milk lowered peak T4 and total exposure by about 8%. The review is explicit that the separation needed for milk is unknown, and that the finding cannot be extended to plant-based milks.

So dairy at breakfast, after the window, is not the thing to lose sleep over. Dairy inside the window — the carton on the way out the door — is.

Source: Levothyroxine Interactions with Food and Dietary Supplements — A Systematic Review.

Source: Levothyroxine — MedlinePlus Drug Information, U.S. National Library of Medicine.

What makes it hard to catch is the lag. Nothing on the carton identifies itself as an interaction, and the effect shows up a week or two later as tiredness or dizziness — by which point you are far more likely to blame your thyroid, your job or your sleep than a drink you started buying last Monday.

If your mornings have changed and you cannot think why, the question worth asking is not only "what am I taking?" but "what am I drinking, and when?"

Does going back to sleep after your dose matter?

Asked often, and the honest starting point is that no study has tested lying down versus staying up after a morning dose. What exists is indirect, and it points reassuringly in one direction.

Levothyroxine is absorbed in the small intestine, and how fast the stomach empties is one of the main things that limits it. That is the reason to suspect that dozing off might slow things down. But the strongest evidence available is about people who sleep for eight hours straight after their dose: those who take it at bedtime.

In a randomised double-blind crossover trial, moving the dose from morning to bedtime improved the numbers — TSH fell by 1.25 mIU/L, free T4 rose by 0.07 ng/dL and total T3 by 6.5 ng/dL. If a full night's sleep after the tablet produced better results than a morning dose, going back to bed for two hours is unlikely to be the thing ruining your levels.

Source: Bolk N et al., Effects of evening vs morning levothyroxine intake: a randomized double-blind crossover trial, Arch Intern Med 2010;170(22). See also a later pragmatic crossover trial in older patients.

If your own levels are off and you cannot explain it, this is still worth raising with your doctor — but it is far down the list, well below coffee, supplements and simply taking it at a different time each day.

What about oat, almond or soy milk?

Two different things decide the answer, and both are printed on the carton.

Added calcium. Plant milks are usually fortified, and many are fortified to match or exceed cow's milk. Cow's milk carries roughly 450 mg of calcium per 355 mL. Read the label and compare: if your oat milk is in the same range, treat it the same way you would treat milk.

Soy is a separate issue. MedlinePlus names soybeans, alongside walnuts and dietary fibre, as things that can affect how levothyroxine works for you, and soy protein taken close to a dose has been reported to interfere with absorption. So soy milk carries a reason for caution that almond milk does not, independent of how much calcium was added to it.

Sources: Levothyroxine — MedlinePlus Drug Information; Levothyroxine Interactions with Food and Dietary Supplements — A Systematic Review, which states plainly that the cow's milk finding cannot be extrapolated to plant-based alternatives.

The practical version: check the calcium figure on your carton, be a little more careful with soy than with the others, and keep whichever one you use after the window rather than inside it.

Questions people also ask

Does black coffee count?

It counts as breaking the empty stomach, which is what the window is protecting. Milk adds calcium on top of that.

What if I forget and eat straight away?

One morning is one morning; it is not a dose you have to make up or double. What is worth doing is noting it, so that if a lab result comes back odd you can see whether that week was unusual. Never change a dose yourself — that is your doctor's call.

Can I take it at bedtime instead?

Some people do, because the requirement is an empty stomach rather than a particular hour, and the evening can be easier to keep clear than the morning. Discuss the switch with your doctor first, and expect the next TSH to be the thing that confirms it worked.
